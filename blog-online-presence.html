Building a Strong Online Presence as a Games Development Student

What is the best approach?

Introduction

Breaking into the games industry is as much about showcasing what you can do as it is about doing it. As a game development student, I've realised that a strong online presence is essential — it's how you display your skills, personality, and passion before you ever meet an employer in person. Whether you want to work for a studio or create indie games, how you present yourself online can make all the difference.

Written By: Callum McGaffin - Games Development Student
Connect: LinkedIn, GitHub, X

Defining Your Online Identity

Before creating any profiles or uploading projects, it's essential to define who you are as a developer. Are you a gameplay programmer? A 3D artist? A designer passionate about storytelling? Knowing your focus shapes everything — from the tone of your posts to the layout of your portfolio.

For me, it all began with a personal portfolio website — a digital hub that gathers my best projects, CV, and links to other platforms. I use a consistent visual theme and username across different sites to strengthen my personal brand. The aim is for someone to recognise my work and understand what I do immediately.

Your online presence should tell a clear story: who you are, what you're good at, and what you love about game development.

Choosing the Right Platforms

Not every platform suits every developer, so choosing the right ones is important. I think of my platforms as tools — each serves a different purpose:

Artstation - showcases visual work such as models, environments, and concept art.
itch.io - hosts playable demos, jam entries, and prototypes.
GitHub - hosts code repositories and technical documentation for your projects.
LinkedIn - connects you with professionals and studios, and highlights your career achievements.
Twitter (X) / Bluesky - allows you to engage with shared communities, share updates, and follow industry trends.
YouTube / TikTok - can help you showcase your projects through gameplay clips or short devlogs.

A key tip for emerging developers is to connect your platforms. For example, your portfolio can link to your itch.io page, GitHub repositories, and LinkedIn profile, making it easy for employers to view all your work.

Promoting Your Work and Getting Noticed

Sharing your work online isn't just for visibility — it's about demonstrating your creative process. Employers and fellow developers want to see how you approach problems, not just the final result.

For example, if I were to upload a new prototype to itch.io, I would include a short development notes section explaining the design decisions behind it — what worked, what didn't, and what I learned. Turning your work into a narrative helps people connect with it on a deeper level.

Game jams are another excellent way to promote skills. They give you finished, playable projects quickly, and the collaborative environment demonstrates teamwork and adaptability — traits studios highly value.

Consistency also matters. Regular updates, small wins, or behind-the-scenes snippets keep your name visible in feeds and enhance credibility.

Curating Your Portfolio: What to Show (and What to Skip)

A well-presented portfolio focuses on quality and clarity, not quantity. The aim is to showcase your strongest, most relevant work while demonstrating professional focus. Think of your portfolio as a curated collection, not an archive — each piece should serve a clear purpose and reflect your current skill level and understanding.

When selecting what to include, prioritise finished or polished projects that clearly showcase your skills. Include collaborative work where your role and contributions are well defined, as this demonstrates teamwork and communication abilities. Personal or independent projects are also beneficial, especially those that emphasise creativity, initiative, or technical innovation.

Equally important is knowing what to exclude. Avoid uploading unfinished prototypes, outdated work, or anything that lacks context or explanation. Employers and collaborators should understand what the project is, what you contributed, and what it demonstrates about your capabilities.

Standing Out from the Competition

In a competitive industry, it's all about highlighting what makes you special. I believe that my strong communication skills and ability to clearly explain projects set me apart. While many developers can code or design, not everyone can share their process as effectively.

Employers frequently seek individuals who can work collaboratively and learn swiftly. Share challenges, feedback, and how you adapted. Authenticity and reflection make your work memorable.

Maintaining and Growing Your Presence

Building an online presence is an ongoing effort. I update my portfolio every few months, especially after new projects or when learning new tools. Even small updates, like changing screenshots or enhancing project descriptions, help keep your portfolio current.

Engaging with your community is equally important. Comment on others' work, share resources, or provide constructive feedback. Many opportunities in game development arise from networking, not just applying. Active participation in Discord servers, Reddit's r/gamedev, or Unreal Engine forums can expand your horizons, deepen your understanding of different workflows, and potentially connect you with others willing to collaborate or network.

Advice for Game Development Students

From conversations with tutors, indie developers, and recruiters, one clear message emerges: make it simple for people to understand what you do and how you do it. Clarity, consistency, and authenticity are highly valuable.

Key Tips
1. Start Early. Build your portfolio while you learning — it evolves with you.
2. Be Consistent. Regular updates show commitment.
3. Collaborate. Team projects teach skills that solo work cannot.
4. Stay Curious. Always keep experimenting with new engines, tools, and styles.
5. Tell Your Story. People connect with authenticity, not perfection.

Final Thoughts

A strong online presence isn't about flaunting — it's about sharing your journey. Every prototype, screenshot, or devlog adds to the story of your growth as a developer. By building an authentic, consistent, and engaging digital identity, you're not just promoting your work — you're showcasing your passion, creativity, and persistence.

Even small projects, when presented thoughtfully, can open big doors.
